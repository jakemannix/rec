# Measurement controls and observed variation

Read-only audit during the extended campaign, 2026-10-22. These observations compare `w64-m64-h64-default-short.json` with the 1K-context portion of `w64-extended-default.json` under `.build/m4-results/`.

Both runs used 64/64/64 workers, automatic QKV scheduling, 2048-token prefill chunks, the default filtered sampler, packet size 8, `MLX_METAL_FAST_SYNCH=1`, and a 2 GiB allocation cache. Their complete production-source SHA-256 dictionaries and generated-kernel lists/hashes are identical. Dependency versions, profile, schedule and execution metadata also match. Startup memory matches exactly: 22,717,343,372 active bytes and 24,595,229,328 peak bytes. There is no evidence that eager parameter materialization or allocation-cache setup changed between these runs.

The intended workload differences were 128 versus 1024 timed decode transitions, three versus five measured repetitions, eight versus sixteen greedy parity steps, and the extended run's preceding exhaustive validator and additional contexts. Both used one warmup. The harness file hashes differ; identical production/kernel hashes establish kernel identity, not complete harness-file identity.

At 1K input context, the short run's filtered packet median was **200.44 tok/s**, with measured range **200.35–200.90**. The extended run's packet median was **168.06 tok/s**, range **165.13–174.16**. Extended scalar samples ranged **147.75–166.07 tok/s**, versus **182.35–184.78** in the short run. All these samples completed their requested lengths without EOS truncation and matched token hashes between variants within each run.

The same 1K prefill workload also slowed: approximately **0.59–0.63 seconds** in the short run versus **0.84–1.05 seconds** in the extended run's measured samples. Both lengths fit the same initial 64K KV-storage bank and use the same prefill chunk. This means decode context growth alone cannot explain all observed timing variation. Extended packet samples increased from approximately 165–168 to 172–174 tok/s over the repetitions; this describes variation without identifying its cause.

The exhaustive validator executes through blocking `subprocess.run(check=True)` before the benchmark loads its model. A process snapshot during measurement showed only the benchmark wrapper and one active benchmark child, with no remaining validator process. There was therefore no observed leftover validator-process contention. The snapshot also showed CPU activity from WindowServer (~26.5%), an Arc renderer (~25.4%), Warp (~20%) and Arc (~15.9%). Those percentages establish concurrent CPU/UI activity, not GPU utilization or proof of GPU contention.

Both recorded host states showed AC power, automatic power mode, macOS 26.6.2, and no recorded `pmset` thermal/performance warning. Physical temperature, GPU clocks and GPU utilization were not measured. No thermal cause is established.

After the paired 36/64 long runs, the interleaved **64, 36, 64, 36** control used 128 transitions and three measured repetitions under otherwise identical settings. Packet medians were **172.62, 158.58, 176.54, 159.91 tok/s**, respectively. All passed exact parity and timed token-chain checks. The 64-worker ranking persisted, while the earlier 200 tok/s absolute result did not. Raw results are archived as `results/control-{1..4}-w{64,36}.json`. These controls separate ranking evidence from absolute timing drift; they do not establish its cause. Report the uncontrolled host-activity limitation and do not attribute the short/extended difference solely to sequence length or thermal throttling.
